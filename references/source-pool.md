# Source Pool

Use this as a source hierarchy, not a rigid whitelist. Prefer the strongest accessible evidence for each factual claim.

## Tier A0: Primary and authoritative evidence

Use first when the source is directly relevant to the claim:

- Company newsrooms, engineering blogs, security advisories, investor relations, earnings releases, and regulatory filings.
- Government agencies, courts, regulators, central banks, statistical offices, exchanges, standards bodies, and international organizations.
- Research papers, conference publications, technical reports, system cards, benchmark documentation, and official repositories.

Common examples include official OpenAI, Anthropic, Google/DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, Apple, Amazon/AWS, GitHub, Cloudflare, SEC, Federal Reserve, ECB, BLS, BEA, Chinese government and regulator sites, UN, and other directly responsible institutions.

A primary source proves what the issuer announced, filed, measured, or claimed. It does not automatically provide independent confirmation of disputed real-world events.

## Tier A1: High-quality independent reporting

Prefer for consequential breaking news and corroboration:

- reuters.com
- apnews.com
- bloomberg.com
- ft.com
- wsj.com
- bbc.com
- nikkei.com / asia.nikkei.com

Use original reporting when possible rather than syndicated copies.

## Tier B: Strong specialist reporting

Useful for technical depth, industry context, and leads that can be verified:

- arstechnica.com
- theverge.com
- techcrunch.com
- wired.com
- technologyreview.com
- caixin.com
- reputable security and semiconductor trade publications with original reporting

Treat vendor access, embargoes, and benchmark claims with normal skepticism.

## Tier C: Discovery radar

Use mainly to discover emerging topics, developer interest, or primary artifacts:

- news.ycombinator.com
- github.com and GitHub Trending
- reddit.com
- x.com
- producthunt.com
- broad aggregators and repost sites

Do not use Tier C alone to support a major factual claim unless the item itself is the primary artifact being reported, such as an author's own post or repository release.

## Beat-specific rules

### AI

Prefer provider announcements, technical reports, system cards, research papers, benchmark documentation, regulator material, and independent evaluations. Label vendor benchmark numbers as vendor claims unless independently reproduced.

### Technology and security

Prefer official release notes, engineering documentation, CVE/CERT/vendor advisories, filings, and original reporting. For vulnerabilities, distinguish disclosure from confirmed exploitation.

### Finance and markets

Prefer exchanges, company filings, central banks, statistical agencies, regulators, and established financial reporting. Never use an old article as a substitute for current market data.

### World affairs

Use official statements to establish what an actor says, and independent reporting to establish what happened. In conflicts and contested politics, separate verified facts, official claims, and unresolved reports.

### China

Prefer official Chinese government, central bank, statistics, regulator, exchange, and company sources for policies and filings, then corroborate consequential interpretation with strong independent reporting. Use Chinese technology outlets mainly for leads and sector context unless they have direct original reporting.
